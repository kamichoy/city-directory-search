1 · Directories

Finds volumes anywhere on the Internet Archive whose title has this name and the word "directory". Results can include other places with the same name, or phone and church directories; remove those with ×.
Query notes: filtering and other collections
  • A collection's ID is the last part of its address: archive.org/details/allen_county is collection:allen_county.
  • Many library collections mix directories with yearbooks, family histories and other genealogy material. Narrow them by title: collection:allen_county AND title:("city directory").
  • Add the city to keep one place only: title:("fort wayne" AND "city directory"). Combine conditions with AND, OR, NOT and parentheses.
  • Limit by year with AND year:[1900 TO 1920]. Volumes with no year in their catalog record are left out by this filter.
  • Filter by title, not subject. Subjects are inconsistent, and many directories are tagged only genealogy.
  • A query loads at most 1,000 volumes, so filter large collections first.
  • Choosing a collection, finding a city, or loading a query replaces the list below. To combine sources, use Add to list for individual volumes.

2 · Search terms

Search tips
  • Put words in quotes to require them to appear together, e.g. "N Meridian" 1234. Case, punctuation and line breaks are ignored, so "N Meridian" also matches N. Meridian.
  • Quoted terms are also much faster. Without quotes the Internet Archive returns every passage containing any one of the words, and common words like N or Meridian can take a minute per volume or time out.
  • The street directory section (usually in the back) is arranged by street and house number. To look up an address, search house number + street, e.g. 1234 N Meridian.
  • Common Polk abbreviations: av avenue, h householder, r resides, wid widow, (c) "colored," used in older directories to designate Black residents.
  • OCR often confuses 0/O and 1/l, and multi-column layouts can run lines together. If nothing comes up, try a shorter term or an alternate spelling.
  • Page links include ?q=, so the viewer highlights your term. The two-page view shows the matching page next to its neighbor.
  • The Scan column shows the scan (image) number, e.g. scan 332, and below it the page number printed in the directory when the Internet Archive has it, e.g. p. 412. Cite copies a citation for that page.
  • A ≈ before the scan number means the page was estimated from the hit's position in the OCR text, because the volume is not in the Internet Archive's search index. The entry may be many pages away; turn pages to find it.
  • In name listings the address comes last, often glued to a code letter: Chung Paul P pres Bamboo Inn Inc r118 W Ohio. OCR then reads r118 as one word, often rll8, which a plain search for 118 misses. OCR-tolerant house numbers catches these and labels them OCR variant. The person at an address is the name before it.
  • When a passage starts right at your address, the tool fetches that page's text to add the line before it, so the excerpt shows whose address it is.
  • Names are listed surname first: search Chung Paul, not Paul Chung. To follow a business and the places tied to it, put the business and each address on their own lines in one search.
  • For many results, use Export CSV, then attach the file to your AI assistant together with Copy for AI ▸ Instructions only. Rows found by several terms are merged into one.
  • If an address doesn't turn up in a year, search the neighboring house numbers on the same street. Open the page where they appear and look for your address in the scan: it may be there but misread, or the page may be folded or damaged in the scan.
  • Copy for AI copies the shown results with instructions for an AI assistant to sort them into a table by year, keeping each hit ID and page link so you can check every row against the scan.
